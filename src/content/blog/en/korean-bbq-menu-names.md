Samgyeopsal, galbi, and bulgogi are useful names to recognize at a Korean meat restaurant, but they do not all describe the same kind of thing. **Samgyeopsal identifies pork belly. Galbi refers to ribs and needs more context. Bulgogi names a prepared dish, rather than one particular cut of meat.** Reading the full menu line helps you distinguish the meat from the way it is served.

## Samgyeopsal: start with pork belly

The Korea Tourism Organization's [samgyeopsal entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/sp/food/ext/special_view.do?menuSn=913&page=1&sContsTtl=&sort=regDt&vcontsId=178546) identifies 삼겹살 as pork belly and describes grilling it. If you want pork belly, this is the word to look for.

The words around it still matter. The same official entry lists **대패삼겹살 (daepae samgyeopsal)** as thin-sliced pork belly and **고추장삼겹살 (gochujang samgyeopsal)** as a red-chili-paste version. Do not assume every samgyeopsal order will be a thick, unseasoned piece of meat.

Compare the complete names before choosing. Two lines containing 삼겹살 can offer different thicknesses or seasoning, even though both use pork belly.

## Galbi: check the meat and the marinade

On a barbecue menu, **소갈비 (sogalbi)** points to beef ribs, while **돼지갈비 (dwaeji galbi)** points to pork ribs. KTO's [marinated beef galbi entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231374) describes beef short ribs in a soy-based marinade. Its separate [pork galbi entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178496) describes pork ribs with either soy-sauce or red-chili-paste seasoning. “Galbi” alone does not settle either question.

You may also see **생갈비 (saenggalbi)** and **양념갈비 (yangnyeom galbi)**. A [KTO restaurant listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=192724) illustrates the distinction: its saenggalbi is grilled without marinade, while its yangnyeom galbi is marinated before grilling. This is an example of menu wording, not a recommendation based on a visit. Here, saenggalbi means ribs for grilling without marinade; it is not an instruction to eat them raw.

If the menu says only 갈비, point to that line and ask which meat it uses and whether it is marinated. Check the sauce separately if its ingredients matter to you.

## Bulgogi: a dish, not a butcher's cut

KTO's [Korean Food Story](https://english1.visitkorea.or.kr/enu/FOD/FO_ENG_1_4.jsp), credited to the Korean Food Promotion Institute, describes beef bulgogi as thin slices marinated in a sweet soy mixture and then grilled. That is a useful example of the preparation, not the name of a single anatomical cut.

The serving method also deserves a question. KTO's [recipe overview](https://english1.visitkorea.or.kr/enu/FOD/FO_EN_EC_Recipes.jsp) notes pan cooking as well as grilling. Ordering 불고기 does not by itself promise a tabletop barbecue setup. Confirm the full dish name, meat, and how it will arrive if you specifically want to grill at the table.

## Keep this comparison beside the menu

| Menu name | What it helps identify | What to check next |
|---|---|---|
| 삼겹살 — samgyeopsal | Pork belly | Thickness and seasoning |
| 소갈비 / 돼지갈비 — sogalbi / dwaeji galbi | Beef ribs / pork ribs | Marinated or unmarinated; sauce |
| 불고기 — bulgogi | A prepared dish; the example above uses sliced, marinated beef | Exact meat and cooking or serving style |

Before you place the order, confirm the amount covered by the listed price, any minimum order, and whether rice is included. These are questions for this restaurant, not facts you can infer from the three names. A familiar dish name is not a complete ingredient list either: ask about marinades and dipping sauces as well as the meat.
