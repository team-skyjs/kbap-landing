Kimchi jjigae and doenjang jjigae are two Korean stews with different starting points. **김치찌개** is built around kimchi; **된장찌개** is seasoned with doenjang, fermented soybean paste. Recognizing those names helps you choose, but neither name provides a complete ingredient list.

## Kimchi jjigae: kimchi is the starting point

The Korea Tourism Organization's [guide to Korean everyday dishes](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=187423) describes kimchi jjigae made with aged kimchi, pork, tofu, and onions. The kimchi brings a tangy character as well as chili seasoning.

That is a common example, not a rule for every restaurant. Read any additional words in the menu name and ask which ingredients are included in the version being served.

## Doenjang jjigae: fermented soybean paste sets the base

[Korea.net's soybean paste stew recipe](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=144788) describes tofu and vegetables seasoned with doenjang. Its illustrated recipe includes beef, mushrooms, and peppers, showing why a tofu-containing stew should not automatically be treated as vegetarian.

The defining difference is the seasoning base. Doenjang jjigae is not simply kimchi jjigae with less chili, and a less red appearance does not establish how spicy it will be.

## A quick menu comparison

| On the menu | Main ingredient or seasoning to recognize | A useful question |
|---|---|---|
| 김치찌개 — kimchi jjigae | Kimchi | What else is in this version? |
| 된장찌개 — doenjang jjigae | Fermented soybean paste | What is in the broth and seasoning? |

Both dishes can contain ingredients that are not obvious from the name. Confirm the restaurant's recipe if you avoid a particular food, and ask about the broth as well as the visible pieces. A translation or a reference recipe cannot verify the bowl in front of you.

## Check how the restaurant sells it

Before ordering, establish whether the listed price is for one serving or a shared dish. Ask whether rice is included and whether there is a minimum order. Those details belong to the restaurant's menu; they are not determined by the word “jjigae.”

If two people want different stews, point to each complete menu name and confirm the quantity separately. If you are ordering one to share, say that explicitly instead of assuming the serving size will make it clear.

## Which should you choose first?

Choose the kimchi-based stew if that is the flavor you want to explore, or the soybean-paste version if you want to try that seasoning. Ask staff about heat and ingredients when those matter more than the name. Neither stew has one universal recipe or spice level.
