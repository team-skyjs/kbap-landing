You have chosen the dish, but the menu still asks you to choose an amount. **1인분 counts a portion, 소·중·대 describes size, and 추가 means an addition.** These words answer different questions. Reading them together with the dish name and price helps you avoid mistaking a larger bowl for another complete order.

## 인분: how many portions?

**인분 (inbun)** measures food by portions per person, according to the National Institute of Korean Language's [Korean Learners' Dictionary](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=70759&nation=eng). Read **1인분** as one portion and **2인분** as two portions.

The word does not give a weight or guarantee how full you will feel. It also does not tell you the restaurant's minimum order: a menu can describe food by the portion without offering a single portion on its own. If you are eating alone, check that separately.

For a line marked 1인분, first establish what its price covers. If a weight is printed beside it, keep that information in view when comparing dishes. At a barbecue restaurant, the meat name and the quantity label have separate jobs; our [guide to Korean BBQ menu names](/blog/korean-bbq-menu-names/) explains the meat side of that distinction.

## 소·중·대: which size?

**소 (小), 중 (中), and 대 (大)** mean small, medium, and large. The official [dictionary entry for 대](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/kor/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=39365) places these words on a size scale and includes an example of ordering a large food serving.

Treat them as size choices for that dish, not fixed numbers of diners. There is no conversion to make from these words alone: 소 does not automatically tell you “enough for two,” and 대 does not tell you “four individual meals.” Ask how many people the restaurant recommends the size for, especially when choosing one dish to share.

If the menu offers several sizes, compare the price beside each one. Do not read the price for 소 and assume it also applies to 대.

## 곱빼기: a larger serving in one bowl

**곱빼기** is useful when you want a larger serving of a dish. Its [official dictionary definition](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/jpn/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=18606&nation=jpn) describes two bowls' worth of food placed in one bowl, with examples including jajangmyeon and jjamppong. That is different from ordering two separate bowls.

Use the word to identify the larger-serving option, then check the restaurant's details. The dictionary meaning does not establish that every ingredient will be exactly doubled or that the price will double. If the distinction matters, ask what increases: the noodles, the other ingredients, or both.

## 추가 and 사리: what are you adding?

**추가** means adding something, as explained in this [National Institute of Korean Language answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=261&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=310692). Read the item next to it to find out what is being added. The word alone does not mean a free refill or something already included in the dish.

For noodles, look for **면추가, 면사리, or 사리추가**. The Korea Tourism Organization's [Extra Noodles guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231545) explains these additions and gives examples such as udon, glass noodles, ramyeon, and thin wheat noodles. 사리 is therefore not a promise that the extra will always be ramyeon. Check which noodle option the menu offers and its additional price.

## Read the amount before choosing the quantity

| What you see | What it tells you | What still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| 1인분 / 2인분 | One / two portions | Amount per portion and minimum order |
| 소 / 중 / 대 | Small / medium / large | How many diners that size suits |
| 곱빼기 | A larger serving in one bowl | What increases and its price |
| 면사리 / 사리추가 | A noodle addition in this context | Noodle type, amount, and extra charge |

Keep the full menu line visible while deciding. A quantity of “one” can refer to one portion, one large dish, or one noodle addition; the label supplies the context.
