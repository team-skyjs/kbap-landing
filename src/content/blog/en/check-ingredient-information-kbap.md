When a Korean menu names a dish but leaves you wondering what goes into it, start by separating two questions: what is this dish generally made with, and what does this restaurant put in its version?

K-Bap can help with the first. For the second, you need information from the restaurant. Here is how to turn the app's ingredient information into a useful conversation before ordering.

## Open the dish, then check the menu name again

K-Bap's [official app description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes opening a recognized menu item to see dish information and ingredients. Start there, but keep the restaurant's original menu alongside it.

Check the whole name, including words that distinguish a topping or variation. If the matched entry appears to describe a different dish, do not use its ingredients as a substitute for the item you actually want. Point to the original menu row when asking staff.

For the scanning steps, see [how to read a Korean menu with K-Bap](/blog/read-korean-menu-with-kbap/). This guide focuses on what to do with the information afterward.

## Treat your avoid list as a starting point for questions

K-Bap lets you select ingredients to avoid and compares dish information with that list. Check that your selections reflect what you want to discuss, then read the ingredient information rather than relying on a label alone.

A risk label does not verify this kitchen's recipe, ingredient substitutions, or preparation. K-Bap's listing explicitly describes its labels as guidance and asks users to confirm with the restaurant. An ingredient missing from a reference entry is not proof that it is absent from the meal.

## Ask about the part of the dish that is still unclear

Instead of asking only whether a dish is “okay,” name the ingredient and the exact menu item. These are example questions to adapt, not answers supplied by the app:

| What you need to establish | A question for staff |
|---|---|
| Whether an ingredient is in the dish | “Does this specific dish contain [ingredient]?” |
| Whether it is part of something less visible | “Is [ingredient] used in the sauce, broth, or seasoning?” |
| Whether a change is possible | “Can it be prepared without [ingredient], or is that already mixed in?” |
| Whether the answer covers the full serving | “Does that include the toppings and the sauce served with it?” |

A request to leave something out still needs an answer. If staff cannot confirm an ingredient, keep it unresolved rather than treating silence as confirmation.

For a food allergy, explain the allergy to staff directly. The UK's [public guidance on eating out with an allergy](https://www.gov.uk/food-allergies) recommends discussing ingredients and kitchen handling with the restaurant. That practical advice is relevant to the conversation; the page's UK legal requirements should not be assumed to apply in Korea.

## Check again on a repeat visit

An earlier meal or saved dish name does not establish what is being prepared today. Keep the same specific question even if you recognize the menu. For example, knowing that you ordered a particular stew last time does not tell you whether today's broth or toppings are the same.

Once the restaurant has clarified the details, use [K-Bap's Korean order card](/blog/show-food-order-in-korean/) to communicate your selection. The card helps show what you are requesting; it does not confirm that the kitchen has accepted a change.
