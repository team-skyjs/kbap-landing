An unfamiliar menu result does not always mean the same thing. The camera may not have captured readable text, the request may have failed, or a readable dish name may not have an exact match in K-Bap's food catalog. Check which problem you have before taking another photo.

## If no menu text was found

Compare the photo with the menu in front of you. Can you read the Korean characters clearly? Move closer to the section you want to order from, keep the names and prices visible, and change your angle if overhead lights reflect off the page. Then use the scan screen to try again.

These are practical ways to make the text easier to see, not a guarantee of recognition. Tiny print, handwriting, and decorative lettering may still be difficult. Before rescanning, read the app's confirmation: replacing a scan can discard the current results and items you have already added to your order.

## If the scan failed

K-Bap distinguishes a scan failure from finding no menu text. If the app asks you to check your connection, address that first; another photograph does not solve a connection problem. Try again after checking that your phone has internet access.

If it keeps failing, stop repeating the same step. Keep the original menu available and ask staff about the item you want. You do not need to invent a dish identity to continue the conversation.

## If you can read the name but the dish is not in the catalog

A translated name and a food record are different things. K-Bap's [official app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes showing food photos for dishes it recognizes. A readable menu line does not guarantee that a corresponding photo or ingredient record is available.

For an unmatched item, the scan result can offer a **Google** search link using the Korean menu name. Treat those results as leads: a restaurant's special may share words with several different dishes. Compare the original Korean text, the restaurant name, and the price rather than choosing the first attractive photograph.

A missing catalog match also means you should not assume that the dish has been checked against your avoided ingredients. Ask the restaurant about the actual recipe before making a decision that depends on those ingredients.

## If the result says “Closest match”

A closest match is a similar catalog dish, not confirmation that the restaurant serves that exact recipe. Open the explanation and compare the original menu name with the suggested dish. The ingredient information and personal rating refer to the suggested catalog dish.

For example, a house special with an extra topping may resemble a familiar dish while using a different sauce or stock. A similar photo can help you ask a better question, but it cannot establish the contents of that serving.

## Before you add the item to your order

Check the original menu name, price, and any size or topping options with staff. If the identity remains uncertain, point to the menu item rather than substituting another dish's name. For ingredients you must avoid, ask directly about the kitchen's ingredients and preparation; an absent warning is not a safety guarantee.
