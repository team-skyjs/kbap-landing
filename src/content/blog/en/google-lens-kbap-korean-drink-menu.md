A Korean drink menu can leave you with two different questions: “What do these words say?” and “Which drink is this?” Google Lens and K-Bap can help in different ways, depending on what you need to check.

## When you need to read the words

Google Lens can translate text in a photo. Take a picture or choose one from your phone, open Lens’s Translate option, select the text, then choose the source and target languages. This is useful when you want a translation of the words printed on a menu, sign, or label. For the current steps, see [Google’s Lens text-translation guide](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?hl=en).

## When you want to check a drink’s product photo

K-Bap scans Korean restaurant menus and shows photos for some menu items it recognizes. Its latest listed iPhone version adds actual product photos for cola, cider, and other drinks on menus. If a drink is recognized and has a catalog photo, that image can help you check which product the menu refers to.

To try it, scan the menu in K-Bap and look for the drink in the translated List view. A product photo may be available for a recognized item; it is not a promise that every drink will have one. If the name or product is still unclear, show the Korean menu line to the staff and ask.

## Which one should you open?

- Choose **Google Lens** when you mainly want to translate visible text from a menu photo.
- Choose **K-Bap** when you want to browse the translated menu and check whether a recognized drink has a product photo.

These tools do not confirm that a drink is currently in stock or verify its price, ingredients, alcohol content, or serving size. Check the restaurant’s menu or ask staff for those details. For help reading the amounts shown on a Korean menu, see [how to read won prices in K-Bap](/blog/read-menu-prices-korean-won-kbap/).
