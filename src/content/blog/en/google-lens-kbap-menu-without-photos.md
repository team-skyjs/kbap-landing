A menu can become readable without becoming easy to choose from. You may understand the translated words and still not know what the food looks like. Google Lens and K-Bap offer different starting points for that problem.

This is a comparison of documented functions and suggested workflows, not a side-by-side accuracy benchmark. We have not measured speed, recognition rates, or translation quality on identical menus.

## Google Lens: start with the text in front of you

[Google Lens](https://lens.google/intl/en-GB_uk/) supports copying and translating text from images. Google's [Android Lens help](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?hl=en-GB) also describes selecting a region or text and choosing the translation option. Entry points and controls can differ by device and app.

For a menu with closely spaced rows, concentrate on the specific line you want to understand. Keep the Korean original available so that you can distinguish nearby items and point out the right one to staff.

You can also use the original dish name for a separate search when you want more context. Treat any photo you find as an example unless it is clearly the restaurant's own image of that exact menu item.

## K-Bap: connect a recognized menu item with food information

K-Bap's [official listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes showing dish photos for recognized items on Korean menus. This can help when a translated name alone does not give you enough context to choose.

The reference photo still has limits. It does not confirm serving size, toppings, meat choice, or how the restaurant presents its version. If no dish is matched, do not assume that another entry with a similar name is an exact substitute.

## Work through the uncertainty in order

| What is still unclear? | Suggested next step |
|---|---|
| The Korean text itself | Translate the relevant line and keep its original wording |
| What kind of food the name refers to | Open available dish information or search the full Korean name |
| Whether this restaurant's version matches the picture | Ask staff about that exact menu entry |
| Whether an ingredient is present | Ask about the recipe, including sauces and broth |

For example, two dishes can share a base name while differing in an important topping. Preserve the full name instead of searching only the word you recognize. This is especially useful when comparing several rows on one menu.

## Pick a workflow, not a winner

Start with Lens if your immediate task is reading text already in front of you. Consider K-Bap when you want food context for a recognized Korean menu item and a way to organize a selection. Neither workflow replaces checking the restaurant's own version before ordering.
