You do not need a menu photo to start learning about Korean dishes in K-Bap. Before choosing a meal, open the food catalog, find a dish that interests you, and read its information.

This is useful when you want to recognize a few food names before arriving at a restaurant. The task is to learn about dishes; a catalog entry does not tell you whether a particular restaurant serves one today.

## Start in the Food tab

Tap **Food** in the bottom navigation. Browse the dish cards and tap one to open its details. You can begin with a photo that catches your attention or a name you have seen elsewhere.

For a broad browse, choose **All** and turn off **Saved** if it is selected. Saved narrows the list to entries you have already kept, which can hide foods you have not discovered yet. You can browse as a guest; saving and personalized filters may prompt you to sign in.

The list also has a sort control. **Name A–Z** and **Name Z–A** can help when you would rather browse by name. The **New** option is not currently selectable, so do not rely on it to find the latest additions.

## Search when you have a name in mind

Tap **Search food…**, enter the dish name, and submit it with the keyboard's search key. Results appear after you submit; typing alone does not run the search.

Use a food name rather than treating this field as a restaurant or address search. Tap a result to open its dish page. If a specific restaurant's full menu name gives no result, try the simpler dish name and compare any result carefully. A similar name does not establish that the entry describes the restaurant's version.

Recent searches provide another way back to terms you have tried. Tapping a recent term runs that search again. You can remove an individual term or use **Clear all** to clear the recent-search list.

## Read beyond the picture

On a dish page, use the description, photos, and any ingredient information provided to understand the entry. The Korean name is also shown when it differs from the displayed name. That gives you something concrete to compare with a Korean menu later.

[K-Bap's official app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes its food photos and ingredient information. Treat those as information about the dish entry, not a promise that every serving will look the same or use the same recipe.

For a short exploration session, open two dishes that interest you and ask:

- What is each dish called in Korean?
- What does the description help me understand about it?
- What would I still need to ask the restaurant?

You might finish with one name you recognize and one question to ask. You do not need to build a large saved list or choose a final order during this step.

## Take that knowledge to the menu

At the restaurant, compare the dish name with the actual menu, including its options and price. Browsing the catalog does not establish availability, portion size, today's recipe, or a restaurant's opening hours.

If you decide to keep a dish for later, [the Saved guide](/blog/save-korean-dishes-kbap/) explains how to save and reopen it. For the next step at the table, [the menu-reading guide](/blog/read-korean-menu-with-kbap/) covers working from a photographed menu.
