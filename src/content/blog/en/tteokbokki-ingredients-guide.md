**Tteokbokki (떡볶이)** is a Korean dish built around chewy tteok, commonly translated as rice cakes. The red, spicy version is familiar street food, but the name covers more than one sauce and combination of ingredients.

When reading a menu, separate three questions: which sauce is used, whether noodles are included, and what else goes into the dish. That gives you a clearer idea of what to expect than the English words “rice cakes” alone.

## The familiar red version

The Korea Tourism Organization's [tteokbokki guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178955) describes rice-cake sticks cooked with gochujang, or red chili paste, and vegetables such as cabbage and green onion. It also notes that fish cake is a frequent addition.

That is a useful starting picture, not a fixed recipe for every shop. In [Korea.net's cooking example](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=191773), the sauce includes sugar and soy sauce as well as gochujang and chili powder; boiled eggs are optional. Sweetness and chili can therefore be part of the same dish. A sweet description does not tell you that the dish will be mild.

The tteok is the central ingredient, but it may share the sauce with several others. Fish cake, egg, or cheese should not be treated as decorative details if you need to know what you are ordering.

## Gungjung tteokbokki: a different sauce and mix

**Gungjung tteokbokki (궁중떡볶이)** is often translated as royal tteokbokki. The official [KTO description](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=180143) uses soy sauce instead of gochujang and includes beef, mushrooms, and vegetables with the rice cakes.

This is a distinct version to recognize on a menu, rather than simply the red dish with less chili. Its soy-sauce base also tells you nothing on its own about whether meat is included: beef is part of the version described by KTO. Check the restaurant's preparation when an ingredient matters to you.

## Rabokki: tteokbokki with ramyeon

**Rabokki (라볶이)** adds instant ramyeon noodles to tteokbokki. KTO's [rabokki entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231494) describes rice cakes, noodles, fish cake, and vegetables in a sweet, spicy sauce. It lists boiled eggs, cheese, and sausages as ingredients that are sometimes added.

The useful distinction is that rabokki already names a noodle-containing dish. You do not have to infer noodles from a photo or assume they are only available as an extra. The particular toppings and amount still depend on the menu.

| Menu name | Main distinction | What to establish at the restaurant |
|---|---|---|
| 떡볶이 — tteokbokki | Often the red chili-paste version | Sauce, spice level, and included ingredients |
| 궁중떡볶이 — gungjung tteokbokki | Soy-sauce version | Meat, vegetables, and the restaurant's preparation |
| 라볶이 — rabokki | Tteokbokki with ramyeon noodles | Which toppings come with the dish |

## Read the whole menu line

Use these names to identify the style, then read the description and options beside it. Ask whether an item shown in a photo is included or sold separately. If the menu uses portion or extra-noodle labels, our [guide to Korean menu portions and extras](/blog/korean-menu-portions-sizes-extras/) explains those terms.

For ingredient questions, keep the exact dish and its sauce in the conversation. A familiar name or a similar-looking photo is not a complete ingredient list.
