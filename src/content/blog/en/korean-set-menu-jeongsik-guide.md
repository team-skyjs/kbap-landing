You recognize the grilled fish on a Korean menu, but a second line offers a grilled fish **정식 (jeongsik)**. Further down, there is a separate price for rice. Before choosing, find out which dishes come together in the meal.

A few menu words help you ask that question. The restaurant's description supplies the details of its particular set.

## Read the dish name and the meal label together

**정식 (jeongsik)** is commonly translated as a set meal. The name before it often identifies the main dish. For example, the Korea Tourism Organization lists both **장어구이 (jangeo gui)**, grilled eel, and **장어정식 (jangeo jeongsik)**, an eel set meal, in its [Naruter restaurant entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=98382). That gives you two different menu choices to compare. Ask what accompanies the eel in the set.

**백반 (baekban)** refers to a meal built around steamed rice, soup and several side dishes. KTO's [guide to baekban meals](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=536&vcontsId=189672) describes versions named after their main dishes, including pork bulgogi and grilled fish. The main dish helps distinguish one meal from another.

**세트 (seteu)** is the Korean rendering of “set.” Read the listed components beside it. A set at one restaurant may combine different items from a set with a similar name elsewhere.

## Check what comes with the meal

Compare the main dish, rice, soup or stew, and any other items the menu explicitly includes. If two meal choices sound similar, the description may explain why their prices differ.

The word **공깃밥 (gonggitbap)** means a bowl of rice, as defined by the National Institute of Korean Language's [Korean Learners' Dictionary](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/m/eng/searchResultView?ParaWordNo=600046&nation=eng&nationCode=6). You may see it offered as an extra. A separate rice price alone leaves a question about your chosen meal. Ask **“밥이 포함되어 있나요?” — “Is rice included?”** before adding a bowl.

When the menu leaves the full combination unclear, point to the meal and ask **“뭐가 같이 나오나요?” — “What comes with it?”** These are practical questions to ask staff. Let their answer establish the current contents, including any changes to the side dishes.

## Then check how much to order

Once you understand the combination, check the serving label and any minimum order. Our [guide to Korean menu portions](/blog/korean-menu-portions-sizes-extras/) explains **인분**, sizes and additions. Keeping the meal's contents and its quantity in view helps you compare the complete order.
