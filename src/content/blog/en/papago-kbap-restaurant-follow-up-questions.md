You show your chosen dishes to restaurant staff, and they reply with a question. Perhaps an item is unavailable, or they need to confirm a portion. Your prepared order is now the starting point for a conversation.

K-Bap can show the dishes and quantities you selected in Korean. Papago can help translate what you want to ask and what the other person says back. This guide focuses on that handover between a prepared list and a follow-up exchange. It compares documented functions and suggests a workflow; it is not a translation-accuracy test or a report of a restaurant visit.

## Keep the order visible, then clarify one question

K-Bap's [official app description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes a Korean order screen for showing selected dishes to the restaurant owner. Use it as the shared list you can return to after a conversation.

If staff point to a dish and say something you do not understand, keep that Korean menu name in view. Avoid guessing whether they mean the quantity, a missing ingredient, or an unavailable item. Ask them to clarify that one entry first.

The card does not send an order to the kitchen or translate a spoken reply. For the steps to prepare it, see [how to show your food order in Korean](/blog/show-food-order-in-korean/).

## Use Papago for the reply as well as your question

NAVER's [Conversation Translation help](https://help.naver.com/service/16448/contents/5971?lang=en&osType=MOBILE) describes a two-person exchange: choose the language pair, open conversation translation, and use the microphone for the language of the person speaking. The other person can then use their language's microphone for the response. Translations can also be played aloud, depending on the voice settings.

Ask whether staff are comfortable speaking into the phone, then take turns. Keep each question short and point to the relevant dish. Read the recognized text when possible before relying on the translated result; if the words do not match what was said, try again.

If you prefer typing, Papago's [text translation instructions](https://help.naver.com/service/16448/contents/17747?lang=en&osType=MOBILE) describe selecting a language pair and entering the phrase to translate. You can show the result and ask staff to type their response if that is convenient for them.

## Three follow-up situations to handle separately

These are example questions to translate or adapt, not tested outputs from either app.

| What needs clarification | A focused question | What to settle before continuing |
|---|---|---|
| Staff indicate that an item is unavailable | “Which item is unavailable?” | Identify the exact menu entry before choosing a replacement. |
| The portion is unclear | “Is this price for one portion?” | Confirm the portion and how many you are ordering. |
| You want a change to the dish | “Can this dish be made without this ingredient?” | Identify the ingredient and wait for a clear answer from the kitchen. |

Keep a replacement separate from an addition. If you change your choice, make sure the final list no longer shows the item you are replacing. A translated suggestion from staff is not automatically a change to your K-Bap selection.

For an ingredient restriction, asking for something to be left out does not establish whether it is already in the stock, sauce, or preparation. If the answer remains unclear, neither the order card nor a translation resolves that uncertainty. Our [ingredient-information guide](/blog/check-ingredient-information-kbap/) explains the difference between a reference dish and the kitchen's actual recipe.

## Return to one final list

After the exchange, check the Korean dish names and quantities again. Point out any replacement, confirm the final price with staff, and make sure the person taking the order has accepted the revised selection. A displayed total is not the restaurant's bill.

Before relying on live conversation translation, check your connection. NAVER's [offline translation guide](https://help.naver.com/service/16448/contents/25501?lang=en&osType=COMMONOS) says conversation translation is not supported offline. Its offline text and voice options have separate language-pack and device requirements; do not assume every translation mode works without data.

[Explore K-Bap](/) for preparing your dish list, and use a conversation tool when you need to understand the reply. Keeping those two tasks clear helps you finish with one agreed order.
