On a wall menu with several columns, understanding the words is only part of the task. You also need to work out which price, size, or extra belongs to the dish you want. A useful goal is to identify one complete menu entry before moving to the next.

Google Lens offers image text selection and translation. K-Bap offers food information for recognized Korean menu items. Here is a suggested way to use those documented functions on a busy board. This is not a side-by-side test: we have not measured either app's accuracy or speed on the same image.

## Start with a complete section

Before opening either app, look at how the board is organized. Find a section heading, a dish you are interested in, and the nearby prices or options. Keep that area together in your photo or reference view.

A tight view of the name alone can leave out the information you need to order. For example, a price beneath a heading may apply to a group of items, while a price beside one row may belong to that row. If the layout leaves that relationship unclear, ask staff instead of guessing from the translated words.

This is an editorial reading strategy, not a claim that either app always interprets a menu's layout correctly.

## Use Lens to focus on the relevant text

Google's [Lens region-selection help](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?hl=en-GB) describes selecting part of an image and translating selected text. Its Android instructions use the Google app: open Lens, take or choose a photo, and select the text or adjust the region box. The help page also describes a Translate view with input and output language controls. Buttons and entry points can differ on other devices.

For a crowded board, start with the section you identified. Check the selection against the original before reading the translation: did you include the full name, or accidentally select a neighboring line? If you need to understand a heading or an option too, select that wording separately while keeping its location in mind.

Google also documents [copying text with Lens](https://search.google/ways-to-search/lens/). Keeping the Korean wording in your own notes can help you return to the same entry after looking up its meaning.

## Use K-Bap when the dish itself is unfamiliar

K-Bap's [official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes menu scanning and photos for recognized dishes, with information about what a dish is and its ingredients. That provides another step when you can read a name but still want food context.

Compare the recognized item with the board before relying on its detail page. Keep any extra words in the menu name in view. A general dish entry or reference photo does not establish the restaurant's portion size, current price, or exact toppings.

If an item is not recognized, leave the match unresolved. Do not replace it with a similar-looking result just to complete the list. For the basic app flow, see [how to read a Korean menu with K-Bap](/blog/read-korean-menu-with-kbap/).

## Reconnect the name, option, and price

Imagine a board with two neighboring dishes and two prices. After translating or opening food information, return to the original board and check:

1. **Name:** Can you point to the exact entry you mean?
2. **Option:** Is there a size, topping, or set choice attached to it?
3. **Price:** Which printed price applies to that choice?
4. **Quantity:** How many servings do you want to request?

This is an illustrative checklist, not an example of output produced by either app. If a detail is still ambiguous, show staff the original entry and ask them to confirm it. Reading a price and knowing which item it belongs to are separate checks.

## Choose the next step by what is missing

Lens can help you select and translate the wording on the board. K-Bap can provide food context when a Korean menu item is recognized. You do not need to use both for every order, and this workflow does not establish that either tool is more accurate.
