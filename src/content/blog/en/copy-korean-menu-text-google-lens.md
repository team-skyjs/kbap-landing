Your friend recommended a dish, but you cannot tell which Korean line on the menu they meant. Sending the name as text lets them quote it in a reply. Google Lens can copy wording from a photo so you can put it into your message.

Once you have chosen your food, K-Bap can help you show the selected dishes to staff on a Korean order screen. Here is a simple way to use each tool for those two moments.

## Copy one menu item in Google Lens

Google documents the same text-selection route in its Google app help for [Android](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) and [iPhone or iPad](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en):

1. Open the **Google app**, tap **Lens** in the search bar, then choose **Search** under the camera search area.
2. Take a photo or choose an existing menu image.
3. Tap the words you need, or adjust the selection box around them, and choose **Select text**.
4. Select **Copy** to put the chosen wording on your clipboard.

Use the complete dish name. Compare the copied characters with the photo before sharing them. If an option belongs to the item you are asking about, include that wording too. Choosing **Copy** keeps this task separate from the **Translate** option in the Lens menu.

## Paste it into a specific question

Switch to your messaging app and paste the name into a message. For example, if you copied **김치찌개**, you could ask:

> Is 김치찌개 the dish you meant, or was it the other stew?

This is an example question you write yourself. You can attach the original photo as context, especially when the menu has several similar choices. Send the message from your messaging app after checking the pasted text.

For a larger menu with crowded columns, our [menu-board guide](/blog/google-lens-kbap-menu-board/) explains how to keep the dish name and its options together while reading.

## Show the order after you decide

K-Bap's [official app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes scanning a menu photo, exploring recognized dishes, and preparing a Korean order screen. Choose dishes in K-Bap and show that screen to staff when you are ready to request them.

The copied text helps you discuss a particular menu line with a friend. The order screen gives staff your selected dishes to read. See [how to show a Korean order card](/blog/show-food-order-in-korean/) for the ordering flow, or [explore K-Bap](/) before your next meal.
