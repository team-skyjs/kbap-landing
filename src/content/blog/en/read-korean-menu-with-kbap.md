A Korean-only menu is easier to work through when you separate three questions: What does the line say? What kind of dish is it? Is this the version you want to order? K-Bap helps with the first two, while the restaurant is the place to confirm the third.

## Start with a clear menu photo

Open K-Bap's scan screen and photograph the menu. Keep the dish names and their prices in the same frame. If light reflects off a laminated page, change the angle before taking the picture. For a crowded menu, concentrate on the section you actually want to read rather than squeezing distant text into the frame.

These are practical photography suggestions, not a promise that every menu will be recognized. A handwritten special or a restaurant's invented dish name can still need a question to staff.

## Read the result alongside the original

Look through the recognized menu items and compare them with the printed menu. Check the price as well as the name: two nearby rows may refer to different sizes or options. If a line seems wrong, use the original menu as your reference rather than ordering from a plausible translation alone.

K-Bap can show a food photo when it recognizes a dish. That picture helps you understand the type of food; it is not a photograph of the restaurant's exact serving. The app's [official listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes this menu-to-dish-photo function.

## Use food information to decide what to ask

Where a matched dish has more information, open it before choosing. If you have ingredients you avoid, review the information available for that dish. A familiar name or a reassuring indicator does not establish what this kitchen puts in its sauce or broth.

For example, the useful follow-up is specific: ask about the ingredient that matters to you, and include the stock or seasoning in the question. If staff cannot confirm it, an app result cannot supply that missing answer.

## Turn your selection into an order

Add the items you want, check their quantities, and open the order card. Compare the names with the menu once more before showing it to staff. The card helps communicate your choice; the restaurant still needs to accept the order and confirm the final price.
