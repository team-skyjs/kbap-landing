You have found an unfamiliar Korean dish in K-Bap and want more context before choosing it. The description tells you what the dish is; the review section gives you comments, ratings, and any photos attached to those entries. Read those together rather than deciding from a star score alone.

This guide covers the reviews attached to a **particular food**. It does not treat every entry as a verified restaurant visit or every photo as the serving you will receive.

## Start with the dish you want to understand

Open **Food**, choose a dish, and scroll to its review section. When reviews are available, tap **Read all** to open that food's review list. A dish without reviews may show an invitation to write one instead.

If you have not chosen a food yet, our [guide to exploring Korean dishes](/blog/explore-korean-dishes-before-visit/) covers browsing and name searches. Here, the useful question is narrower: what information do the entries for this dish actually add?

Read the comments alongside the dish description. Look for specific details you want to clarify, such as texture or how the food was served. A brief rating without an explanation gives you less context than a comment describing what the rating refers to.

## Use the country toggle as a filter

For a signed-in account with a nationality set, the review list can show a toggle such as **US only** or **KR only**. Its country comes from your account nationality. It is not a selector for any country, and it does not use your current travel location. Guests and accounts without that value do not see the toggle.

Turning it on narrows the entries to that country and changes the displayed rating summary to the corresponding group. If the filter returns no reviews, turn it off to return to the broader list. An empty filtered result does not mean the food has no reviews at all.

The country filter is a way to narrow what you read, not a promise that the comments will match your taste. If **Load more reviews** appears, use it to read additional entries; the cards currently on screen need not represent the full review count.

## Read the text and open the photos

When **Show translation** appears above a review, tap it to read the translated text. After translation, the label becomes **Translated from …** or **Translated**. Tap that label again to return to the original. A review already identified as being in your app language may have no translation control.

If a review includes photos, tap a photo to open a larger view. Not every entry has text or photos, so use the information actually provided. A photo can give you a question to ask about presentation or portion size; it does not establish what a different restaurant will serve today.

## Turn the review into a useful question

For example, a comment about a soft texture may help you decide to ask how the dish is prepared. A photo showing a shared plate may prompt a question about how many people that restaurant's portion serves. These are examples of how to use the information, not findings from a restaurant test.

Keep the final check with the restaurant: its recipe, available options, and portion size can differ from an app entry. For menu labels such as **인분** or **대**, see our [Korean menu portion guide](/blog/korean-menu-portions-sizes-extras/).
