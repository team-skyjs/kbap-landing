A Korean menu may list **김치만두** beside **군만두**, but those names tell you different things. One points to an ingredient in the filling; the other describes how the dumplings are cooked. Reading both parts helps you ask for the version you want.

**만두 (mandu)** are Korean dumplings. The Korea Tourism Organization's [mandu introduction](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178964) describes thin wrappers enclosing fillings such as meat, chives and tofu, with steaming, pan-frying and soup among the preparations.

## Start with the filling name

**고기만두 (gogi mandu)** means meat dumplings. KTO uses this name and translation in its [Midohyang restaurant entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=192318). The word identifies a meat filling without telling you every ingredient or which meat the kitchen uses.

**김치만두 (kimchi mandu)** contains kimchi in the filling. It can contain meat too: KTO's [kimchi mandu description](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=180144) gives a version with kimchi, pork, tofu and glass noodles. If you want to know what is inside, ask about the full filling rather than relying on the word kimchi alone.

These names also leave the cooking method open. Look for another label or ask how that particular order will be prepared.

## Then check the preparation

| Menu name | What it describes |
|---|---|
| **찐만두 (jjin mandu)** | Steamed dumplings. KTO lists this translation in its [Gaecheon Sikdang entry](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=190264). |
| **군만두 (gun mandu)** | Fried dumplings. KTO's [gun mandu guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231387) illustrates a pan-fried version with a crisp exterior. |
| **만둣국 (mandutguk)** | Dumpling soup. KTO's [mandu soup guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178155) describes dumplings cooked in broth. |

A steamed or pan-fried label does not identify the filling. For soup, check the dumplings and the broth separately if an ingredient matters to you. Recipes and accompaniments can vary between restaurants.

## Check the portion before ordering

Once you have chosen the filling and preparation, look for the number of dumplings in an order and whether the menu offers a plate or a soup. Point to the item and ask **“몇 개 나오나요?” — “How many pieces come with it?”** when the quantity is unclear.
