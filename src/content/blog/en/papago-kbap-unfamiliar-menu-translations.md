You have translated a Korean menu, but one item still means little to you. Perhaps you can read the name aloud without knowing what arrives in the bowl. Or the description says “mixed,” without explaining what is mixed.

Before translating the same words again, identify the missing information: the original text, the type of food, or a restaurant-specific option. Papago and K-Bap can help at different points in that process.

This guide uses official feature descriptions and an official tourism food guide. It is not a test of the apps' translation accuracy, and the food example below is not a captured result from either app.

## Keep three versions of the name together

Consider **돼지국밥**, written as **dwaejigukbap** in the Korea Tourism Organization's [guide to Busan's pork and rice soup](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=904&vcontsId=221285).

| Form | What it helps you do |
|---|---|
| 돼지국밥 | Find and point to the original Korean menu entry |
| Dwaejigukbap | Recognize the dish name written in Latin letters |
| Pork and rice soup | Begin to understand the kind of meal |

These forms serve different purposes. The Latin-letter name can be unfamiliar without being a translation error. The short English description gives useful context, but it does not describe every restaurant's version.

## Use Papago to isolate the wording you need

Papago's [mobile image translation help](https://help.naver.com/service/16448/contents/5973?lang=en&osType=MOBILE) describes choosing a language pair, taking a photo or selecting one from your gallery, and highlighting the phrase to translate.

For this task, select the complete menu entry, including any words or parentheses beside the main dish name. Keep a photo of the surrounding menu too. That lets you return to the correct row if two entries appear similar after translation.

If you understand “pork and rice soup” but not an additional option, focus your next question on that option. Repeating the base name alone leaves out the part you are trying to resolve.

## Use K-Bap for food context when a dish is recognized

K-Bap's [official app description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes photos and food information for recognized dishes on Korean menus. Open the available dish information and compare it with the original menu entry.

A reference entry can help you understand the general dish. It may not explain a restaurant's special name, extra topping, or combination. If no suitable match appears, keep the original name and ask staff; a similar-looking entry is not confirmation.

These are suggested uses of documented functions, not exclusive capabilities or a claim that K-Bap translates a difficult name better than Papago.

## Turn “mixed” into a specific question

The VISITKOREA guide describes pork-soup menus with meat, offal, sundae, and combinations. Its examples show why understanding the main dish does not settle the contents of every option.

Point to the exact row and ask: “What is included in this mixed version?” Then confirm whether that is the version you want. For ingredients that matter to you, ask about the actual preparation rather than treating the short translated name as a complete recipe.

Before moving on, you should be able to connect the original row, the dish description, and the option you chose.
