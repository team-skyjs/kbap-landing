Bibimbap is a rice dish with toppings that you mix together before eating. It is a useful name to recognize on a Korean menu, but the word alone does not tell you every ingredient in the bowl.

## What usually goes into bibimbap?

A bowl can include cooked rice, prepared vegetables, egg, and meat, with seasonings such as gochujang, a red chili paste, and sesame oil. The Korea Tourism Organization's [Jeonju bibimbap guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=221301) describes vegetables, beef, and egg among the toppings. The same guide describes the separate preparation of several vegetable toppings.

Treat that as an introduction to the dish, not a fixed recipe that every restaurant follows. The most useful information is the description beside the specific item you plan to order.

## Check the version, not just the name

The Korean spelling is **비빔밥**. Keep any words before it when reading or translating a menu: they may distinguish one version from another.

One important example is **육회비빔밥**, yukhoe bibimbap, which includes raw beef. The [official Jeonju guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=221301) distinguishes that variation from versions using cooked beef. Do not assume that an attractive food photo tells you whether the meat or egg in a particular serving is cooked.

If you are comparing two menu rows, point to the complete name. Asking about “bibimbap” generally may produce an answer about a different bowl.

## Three questions worth asking before you order

- **What toppings come with this version?** Ask about meat and egg explicitly if those affect your choice.
- **Is the sauce already mixed in or served separately?** If you prefer to adjust the amount, ask whether that is possible before the kitchen prepares it.
- **Can this ingredient be left out?** A request is not confirmation. Wait for staff to say whether the change can actually be made.

A vegetable-filled bowl is not enough evidence to call a dish vegetarian or suitable for an allergy. Sauces, seasoning, and preparation also matter; confirm your particular requirements with the restaurant.

## When the bowl arrives

Bibimbap is intended to be mixed. If sauce is provided separately, start with a little and adjust to your taste. Before mixing, check that the bowl matches the version you ordered; changes are harder to discuss once everything is combined.
