A restaurant sends you a menu image, or you save a screenshot while planning dinner. You do not need another phone to photograph that screen. **Google Lens can translate text in a saved image, and K-Bap can scan a menu image selected from your photo library.** Choose the next step according to what you need to understand, then contact the restaurant to arrange the actual takeout order.

## Start with the original menu image

Keep the restaurant's name and the page or message the image came from. A screenshot preserves what was visible when it was taken; it does not confirm today's prices, opening hours, or availability.

Save the readable menu image rather than a screenshot of several tiny thumbnails. If the restaurant sent several pages, keep them together in your photo library so you can return to the right page. These are preparation tips, not a claim that either app can recover missing or unreadable text.

## Open the saved image in Google Lens

Google documents this route in the Google app for both [Android](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) and [iPhone or iPad](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14516623?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en):

1. Open the **Google app** and tap the Lens icon in the search bar.
2. Choose **Translate**, then select the menu from **Screenshots** or **All images**.
3. Select the words you want to read and choose the input and output languages.

You can translate all the text or a selected part. Lens also offers text selection and copying. Keep the original Korean item name with any note you make, so the restaurant can identify the item even if the English wording is unfamiliar.

These steps refer to the Google app. They are not instructions for a Google Lens button inside K-Bap or Apple's Photos app.

## Open the saved image in K-Bap

In K-Bap, open the menu scan screen and use the gallery or choose-photo control. Sign in if prompted, then select one menu image from your library. For a menu with several pages, work through one image at a time.

This uses K-Bap's menu scan flow. For recognized dishes, its [current app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/k-bap/id6788635067) describes translated names, food photos, and ingredient information. That context can help when you can read a name but still do not know what the dish is.

Compare the recognized dish with the original entry. A reference photo is not a preview of that restaurant's takeout box. If a match is missing, follow [what to do when K-Bap does not identify a menu item](/blog/kbap-menu-item-not-identified/) instead of choosing a different dish just to fill the gap.

## Turn your reading into a pickup request

Reading the menu is preparation. Before treating your meal as ordered, confirm the details with the restaurant through the contact method it provides:

- **Takeout:** Is the dish available for pickup today?
- **Order:** Which exact item, option, and quantity are you requesting?
- **Timing:** When should you collect it?
- **Total:** What is the current total for that order?

For example, you might ask whether two servings of the dish shown in your screenshot are available to collect at 7 p.m. That is an illustrative request, not a live availability check or an order submitted by either app. Wait for the restaurant's confirmation.

K-Bap can also prepare a Korean order screen to show staff. See [how to show a food order in Korean](/blog/show-food-order-in-korean/) for that in-person step. Preparing the screen does not send a takeout order to the restaurant.

Use Lens when you need to read the saved wording; use K-Bap when you want menu-focused information about a recognized dish. Neither workflow here verifies a restaurant's current pickup arrangements. [Explore K-Bap](/) before your next Korean menu encounter.
