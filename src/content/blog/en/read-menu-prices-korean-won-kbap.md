Suppose a menu lists **김치찌개 12,000원**. That line says the kimchi stew costs 12,000 Korean won. On a K-Bap scan, you may see **₩12,000** alongside a price in a more familiar currency. Both numbers refer to the same dish. You do not add them together.

## Start with the number in won

**원** is the Korean word for won, and **₩** is its symbol. The Korea Tourism Organization introduces the currency in its [guide to Korean money](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoHtmlView.do?vcontsId=140040).

Match the price in the scan to the printed menu line. In the stew example, first check that 12,000원 belongs to the stew itself. A price on the next line might be for extra noodles or a larger size. Our [guide to portions, sizes and extras](/blog/korean-menu-portions-sizes-extras/) explains the Korean words that tell you what a price covers.

## Choose your display currency

In the English interface, sign in, open **Profile**, and tap **Edit**. On the **Edit profile** screen, select **Currency**. Search by name or code, such as **Canadian Dollar** or **CAD**, select the currency, then tap **Save changes**. To return to the currency associated with your profile nationality, select **Automatic** and save.

This preference helps you read prices in a familiar unit. The restaurant's menu still lists its own price in won. If you want the app text in a different language, see [K-Bap's language settings](/blog/kbap-language-at-korean-restaurant/).

## When the scan shows only ₩

If K-Bap recognizes a won price and the scan supplies a usable exchange rate, the list can show a converted amount beside it. A won price can also appear on its own. For example, a scan with no available rate omits the conversion, and a Korean won result needs no second amount. If you expected Canadian dollars, check your saved **Currency** setting first. You can still compare the prices in won.

Before ordering, check the price and quantity against the current menu. On the order screen, **Estimated total** adds the recognized won prices for the quantities you selected. An item with no recognized price contributes nothing to that estimate; the restaurant may still charge for it. Use the conversion for a rough comparison and the restaurant's bill for the actual amount due.
